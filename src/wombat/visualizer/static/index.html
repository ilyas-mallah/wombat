How the Wombat visualizer works

Wombat is a small recurrent transformer. It reads the board once, then runs one shared reasoning block over a scratchpad again and again before it reads out a move and a value. The number of repetitions, T, is chosen when you run it, so one checkpoint can answer at once (T = 0) or think longer (up to T = 32). Everything runs on this computer's CPU.

board→encoder→ reasoning block × T→move probabilities + value

The three modes

  • Play: pick your colour and press New game. Click a piece, then a highlighted square (a picker appears for promotions). Undo takes back your last move and Wombat's reply. Choose a Stockfish level under Wombat plays against to watch Wombat play Stockfish instead: Stockfish uses UCI_LimitStrength with that UCI_Elo (1320 is its minimum), 100 ms per move and one thread. Those games play to the end and the Match results card keeps Wombat's wins, draws and losses per level.
  • Puzzles: a random puzzle from the held-out test split. Wombat plays the solver's side after each pause. A green check means its move matched the solution (or mated), a red cross means it did not. The green arrow then shows the right move, which is played so the line can continue with the opponent's reply. Retry runs the same puzzle again, which is the quickest way to compare two values of T.
  • Watch: Wombat plays both sides for the chosen number of plies, or plays Stockfish at a low node count. With auto-continue a new position starts when a game ends.

Reading Wombat's view

  • The blue arrow is the move Wombat is about to play.
  • Candidates: the five most likely moves, with Wombat's probability for each among the legal moves. The top one is the move it plays.
  • Evaluation: Wombat's value head predicts the win chance W of the side to move. It is shown in pawns from White's side, like Stockfish, by inverting the Lichess curve: pawns = ln(W / (1 - W)) / 0.368208 with W clamped to 0.001..0.999. The Wombat bar left of the board shows it for the current position at the current T, and each candidate's eval column is Wombat's per-move value after that move.
  • Value head untrained: puzzle-trained checkpoints (all runs/v6-*) only learned to pick moves, so their evaluations would be noise. The page shows "value head untrained" and a striped bar instead of a number; their moves are still meaningful.
  • Snapshots: game-training runs save snapshot-step-*.pt files every few thousand steps. They appear under their run in the checkpoint list, so earlier iterations can be played.
  • Top move after T steps: the same forward pass read out after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 steps. If the move changes along the strip, thinking longer changed Wombat's mind. In puzzle mode the chips are green when that depth would have found the solution and red when it would not.
  • Moving the T slider or switching checkpoint re-reads the last analysed position at once, so you can compare depths or checkpoints on the same position without replaying anything.

Explaining a move

  • Click any move in the move list, or press Explain last move, to ask the chosen checkpoint why it would play that move at the current T. The board shows the position before the move until you press Back to the game; a running game carries on meanwhile. Changing T or the checkpoint re-explains.
  • By reasoning depth: Wombat's top move after 0 to 16 steps, and its probability for the explained move.
  • Values are Wombat's evaluation of the position and its per-move values, from White's side. The amber arrow is the likeliest legal opponent reply to its top move, scored by its lookahead heads, on checkpoints that have them.
  • Attention: blue shading shows how much the moving piece's square token attended to each square in the last block of the last step.
  • Remove one piece: every other piece except the kings is taken off in turn (skipping removals that make the position invalid or the move illegal) and Wombat is asked again at the same T. The badges show how many points the explained move's probability changes; the list says what it would then play.

Stockfish evaluation (optional)

  • Off by default. When on, Stockfish runs on one CPU thread at about 20k nodes per position.
  • The SF bar, next to Wombat's, is White's win chance from Stockfish's centipawn score, using the Lichess formula win% = 50 + 50 * (2 / (1 + exp(-0.00368208 * cp)) - 1). The number on the bar is the score in pawns, or Mn for mate.
  • Each move is labelled by the win chance it gives up compared with Stockfish's best move: ?! inaccuracy from 5 points, ? mistake from 10, ?? blunder from 15. Hover a move to see the exact loss and the best move.
  • Accuracy per move is the Lichess formula 103.1668 * exp(-0.04354 * loss) - 3.1669, clamped to 0..100, and is averaged under the move list for each player.
